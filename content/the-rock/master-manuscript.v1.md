# WELCOME TO THE ROCK.

KOH SAMUI · THAILAND

Beautiful beaches. Serious fighters. Incredible food. Questionable driving decisions.

This isn't your average tourist brochure. It's the stuff we wish somebody told you before you got here.

Welcome to Koh Samui.

Beautiful beaches, incredible food, world-class Muay Thai, and enough surprises to remind you that you're a long way from home.

Listen, we're glad you're here. But this isn't one of those tourist brochures where everything is sunshine, coconuts, and somebody trying to sell you a boat excursion.

We're going to tell you how things actually work around here.

Where to go. What to avoid. How to respect the culture. How to get around without turning your vacation into an expensive life lesson.

Consider this a little advice from the people who call this island home.

Enjoy yourself. Respect the island. Keep your head on straight. And leave the ego in the ring.

# 01 // KNOW THE ROCK
## THE NEIGHBORHOODS, THE ISLANDS, AND WHERE YOU ACTUALLY ARE

### FIELD REPORT 001-01 // LAMAI — WELCOME TO OUR NEIGHBORHOOD
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

This is our side of the island, and we like it that way.

Lamai has a little bit of everything. Beaches, restaurants, markets, local businesses, nightlife, and a strong Muay Thai community.

It's got personality. You'll meet fighters, trainers, families, travelers, and people who've been living here longer than you've probably been alive.

Here's some advice: don't spend your entire visit walking the same tourist strip. Take a look around. Try a neighborhood restaurant. Visit the local markets. Get to know the people.

There's a lot more to Lamai than what you see from the main road.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Be curious, support local businesses, and respect residential neighborhoods.

### FIELD REPORT 001-02 // CHAWENG — THE ISLAND TURNS UP THE VOLUME
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

You want nightlife? Congratulations. You found it.

Chaweng is one of Samui's busiest tourist areas, with beach clubs, shopping, bars, restaurants, and plenty happening after dark.

Nothing wrong with enjoying yourself. Just understand what you're getting into.

Watch your belongings, arrange a safe ride home, and don't confuse being on vacation with being invincible.

And if you're training tomorrow morning, remember that your trainer is still expecting you to show up.

**FIELD NOTE:** You want nightlife? Congratulations. You found it. Just remember, if you're training at 8 AM tomorrow, the gym doesn't accept “but the DJ was really good” as a medical excuse.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Arrange safe transport home and manage your training and recovery.

### FIELD REPORT 001-03 // BOPHUT, FISHERMAN'S VILLAGE & MAENAM — SLOW IT DOWN
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

Want a change of pace?

Head north.

Fisherman's Village offers waterfront dining, shops, and a more polished evening atmosphere. Maenam has quieter stretches and a more residential feel.

Good places to spend an afternoon or evening when you want something different from Lamai.

### FIELD REPORT 001-04 // NATHON & LIPA NOI — THE OTHER SIDE OF SAMUI
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

The west coast has its own personality.

Nathon is one of the island's administrative and transport centers, with local shops, ferry connections, and everyday Thai town life.

Lipa Noi is known for its quieter coastline and sunset views.

Not every part of Samui needs to entertain you. Sometimes the best experience is seeing how people actually live.

### FIELD REPORT 001-05 // KOH PHANGAN — MORE THAN THE FULL MOON PARTY
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

Everybody knows about the Full Moon Party.

But reducing an entire island to one beach party is like saying New York is nothing but Times Square.

Phangan has quiet villages, beaches, hiking, wellness retreats, and beautiful coastal areas.

There's a lot more going on once you get away from Haad Rin.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Check ferry service, accommodation and weather before traveling.

### FIELD REPORT 001-06 // KOH TAO — DIVING TERRITORY
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

If you're interested in scuba diving or snorkeling, Koh Tao deserves a look.

It's known for dive schools, underwater scenery, and a laid-back island atmosphere.

Just remember that the roads and terrain can be steep. Don't assume every island road is an easy ride.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Choose qualified dive operators and plan transport carefully.

### FIELD REPORT 001-07 // ANG THONG MARINE PARK — WORTH GETTING OFF THE ISLAND FOR
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

Limestone islands, emerald water, dramatic viewpoints, and beautiful scenery.

It's one of the area's standout day trips.

But here's the deal: check the marine forecast and listen to the boat operator. Rough seas don't care how much you paid for your excursion.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Verify access, weather and operator arrangements.

### FEATURE // LAMAI VS. CHAWENG — SAME ISLAND, DIFFERENT UNIVERSE
**[NEW DRAFT]**

Lamai and Chaweng are both Samui, but they don't always feel like the same conversation.

Lamai is where you can finish training, grab food, and settle into a rhythm. Chaweng is where the island turns the volume up and occasionally forgets where the volume button is.

Neither place is automatically better. The question is what kind of day you want.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Choose the area that fits your plans and arrange safe transport.

# 02 // HOW THIS PLACE WORKS
## A LITTLE HISTORY, A LITTLE ECONOMICS, AND A LOT OF PERSPECTIVE

### FIELD REPORT 002-01 // BEFORE THE RESORTS, THERE WERE COCONUTS
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

Believe it or not, Koh Samui wasn't always the international destination you see today.

Long before beach clubs and luxury villas, coconut farming and fishing were major parts of island life.

Traveling across Samui used to be much harder, and the development of roads and modern transportation changed everything.

So when you're enjoying your air-conditioned room, take a second to appreciate how much this place has changed within a relatively short period.

### FIELD REPORT 002-02 // WHY FLYING HERE CAN BE EXPENSIVE
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE · FACT CHECK BEFORE PUBLICATION**

Ever look at a flight from Bangkok to Samui and think, “Hold on, why does that cost so much?”

Fair question.

Samui Airport is privately owned and operated by Bangkok Airways, and the island has fewer direct-flight options than many mainland destinations.

That's part of why flights can be expensive.

The alternative is usually traveling through Surat Thani and taking a ferry.

Cheaper sometimes? Sure.

More convenient? Depends how much you enjoy carrying luggage between buses and boats.

**FIELD NOTE:** Ever look at a flight from Bangkok to Samui and wonder whether they're charging you for the airplane or selling you a small percentage of it? Welcome to island aviation economics.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Compare total journey cost, transfer time, weather and ferry schedules.

### FIELD REPORT 002-03 // RESPECT THE PEOPLE WHO KEEP THE ISLAND RUNNING
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

Here's something worth remembering.

The person cleaning your room, cooking your food, repairing a building, or working behind the scenes might be Thai or a migrant worker from a neighboring country, including Myanmar.

Tourism depends on a lot of people whose work visitors rarely notice.

Treat them with dignity.

Learn to say thank you. Be patient when there's a language barrier. Don't act like spending money gives you permission to disrespect somebody.

That shouldn't be complicated.

### FEATURE // FROM COCONUT PLANTATIONS TO AN INTERNATIONAL AIRPORT
**[NEW DRAFT]**

Samui didn't wake up one morning covered in resorts. Its transformation involved roads, transport, land use, local businesses, workers, and generations of people adapting to change.

It's easy to see the beachfront restaurants. It's harder to notice the systems behind them.

That's why this field manual isn't only about what to do. It's about where you are.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Enjoy modern Samui while respecting the communities that support it.

# 03 // RESPECT THE CULTURE
## YOU'RE A GUEST IN SOMEONE ELSE'S COUNTRY

### FIELD REPORT 003-01 // JAI YEN — KEEP A COOL HEART
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

One of the most useful Thai expressions you'll learn is jai yen, meaning something like keeping a cool heart.

Here's the East Coast translation:

Getting louder doesn't automatically make you right.

If something goes wrong, stay calm. Explain the situation. Give people room to help you.

Thailand places considerable importance on respectful communication and maintaining composure.

That doesn't mean you can't stand up for yourself.

It means you can handle business without turning every disagreement into a public performance.

**DON'T BE THAT GUY // THE RESTAURANT ARGUMENT [NEW DRAFT]**

“The food is taking fifteen minutes. Time to announce my frustration to the entire street.”

Congratulations. Everybody can hear you. That still doesn't make the kitchen move faster.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Speak respectfully, explain the problem calmly and listen to the response.

### FIELD REPORT 003-02 // TEMPLES AREN'T TOURIST PROPS
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

Thailand's temples are active religious and cultural spaces.

Dress respectfully. Cover your shoulders and knees where required. Remove your shoes when entering designated buildings.

Don't climb on sacred structures or pose disrespectfully with Buddha images.

And if monks are present, be mindful of their religious customs. In Theravada Buddhist practice, monks generally avoid physical contact with women.

You're welcome to appreciate the culture. Just remember that other people are there to practice their faith.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Follow posted temple rules and ask before photographing people.

### FIELD REPORT 003-03 // THE HEAD, THE FEET, AND PERSONAL SPACE
**LOCAL KNOWLEDGE**

In Thai culture, the head is traditionally considered a particularly respected part of the body, while feet are considered low.

Avoid touching people's heads without permission.

Don't point your feet toward Buddha images, monks, or people in a disrespectful way.

And don't use your feet to move objects that somebody would normally handle with their hands.

Little things matter.

### FIELD REPORT 003-04 // RESPECT YOUR KRU
**CAMP ADVICE**

Now we're talking about something close to home.

At Lamai Muay Thai, your trainers aren't just people holding pads for you.

They're teaching an art with deep cultural roots.

Listen when they correct you. Show up ready to learn. Respect their experience.

You might have trained for years back home. That's great.

But if a trainer who's spent a lifetime around Muay Thai tells you to adjust something, maybe hear them out before explaining how your old gym did it.

You came all this way to learn.

Give yourself that opportunity.

### FIELD REPORT 003-05 // WAI KRU & RAM MUAY
**[NEW DRAFT] · CAMP ADVICE**

Muay Thai has traditions that go beyond punches, kicks, knees, and elbows. Wai Kru and Ram Muay are associated with showing respect to teachers and the art.

You don't need to arrive knowing every custom. You do need to arrive willing to learn.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Ask your trainer about the traditions you encounter and follow camp guidance.

# 04 // STREET SURVIVAL
## THE STUFF THAT CAN SAVE YOU MONEY, TROUBLE, AND A TRIP TO THE HOSPITAL

### FIELD REPORT 004-01 // SCOOTERS — LEAVE YOUR EGO AT THE GYM
**CAMP ADVICE · SAFETY REVIEW REQUIRED**

**Welcome to Samui. Everybody's Got Two Wheels and an Opinion.**

Listen, we're going to explain something that might save you a whole lot of money, embarrassment, and possibly a trip to the hospital.

You're going to see scooters everywhere.

Locals riding scooters. Tourists riding scooters. Families riding scooters. People carrying enough groceries to feed an entire neighborhood on a motorcycle that looks like it's been through three generations.

Welcome to Thailand.

And yes, you're probably going to see people riding without helmets.

Does that mean you should?

Absolutely not.

Here's the reality: you can physically get on a scooter and ride around the island without a helmet. We're not standing outside your accommodation conducting helmet inspections.

But understand something.

**Just because you can get away with something doesn't mean you're protected from the consequences.**

The pavement doesn't care whether you're a tourist, a professional fighter, or somebody who watched three motorcycle tutorials on YouTube.

And neither does the insurance company.

#### THE HELMET SITUATION — DON'T CONFUSE WHAT YOU SEE WITH WHAT'S LEGAL

Thailand requires motorcycle riders and passengers to wear helmets.

Now, enforcement hasn't always looked consistent everywhere. You'll see locals without helmets, tourists without helmets, and people making decisions that would give a driving instructor a headache.

But don't mistake what you see for permission.

**OCTOBER 2026 // LOCAL ENFORCEMENT NOTE — UNVERIFIED; DO NOT PUBLISH AS OFFICIAL FACT**

Around Samui, riders have been reporting increased attention to helmet compliance and traffic enforcement, including checks affecting tourists and foreign residents. Treat this as a local observation that needs confirmation against current police notices before publication.

And here's where people get themselves into trouble.

Somebody sees a local riding without a helmet and thinks, “Well, they're doing it.”

My friend, that person is not your legal advisor.

They are not paying your fine.

They are definitely not covering your hospital bill.

Wear the helmet.

It's a lot cheaper than finding out the hard way.

#### THE RING ROAD — THIS IS NOT YOUR PERSONAL RACETRACK

Samui has a main ring road connecting much of the island.

It's convenient. It's scenic. And depending on the conditions, it can be an absolute circus.

You've got scooters, cars, delivery vehicles, pickup trucks, songthaews, pedestrians, unexpected turns, and the occasional driver who apparently received their license in a cereal box.

Then it rains.

Now you've got wet pavement, loose gravel, sand, oil, steep hills, and corners that deserve considerably more respect than they're getting.

Here's the part people forget.

You might be an excellent driver back home.

Great.

But driving experience doesn't automatically prepare you for unfamiliar roads, different traffic habits, and tropical weather.

**The island doesn't care about your confidence. It cares about your traction.**

#### YES, YOU CAN RENT A BIG MOTORCYCLE. BUT WHY?

Let's address something else.

You're not limited to little scooters.

Depending on the rental company and availability, you may find larger motorcycles, touring bikes, and high-performance sport bikes.

You want a Ducati?

Somebody may be willing to rent you one.

And listen, we appreciate a beautiful motorcycle as much as anybody.

But respectfully, what exactly are we trying to accomplish here?

You're on a tropical island, not qualifying for MotoGP.

You're going to encounter traffic, pedestrians, unpredictable road surfaces, and stretches where a fraction of that engine's capability is already more than enough.

You've got enough torque to launch yourself into next Tuesday, and you're using it to ride eight minutes to get an iced coffee.

Congratulations. Very impressive.

The coffee is still 80 baht.

A bigger engine doesn't make the road wider, improve the weather, or give you better judgment.

If you're an experienced rider with the proper license, insurance, and equipment, that's your decision.

Just don't confuse horsepower with common sense.

**Nobody at Lamai is awarding bonus points because you arrived at training on a superbike.**

*Editorial note: the coffee price is illustrative humor, not a verified price.*

#### RENTING A BIKE — PROTECT YOURSELF BEFORE YOU TURN THE KEY

Before riding away from a rental shop, take photographs and video of the entire motorcycle.

Check the brakes, tires, lights, mirrors, and existing damage.

Understand the rental agreement.

Confirm what insurance actually covers, including whether you have the correct license and any required international driving permit.

And be careful about handing over your original passport as collateral. Ask whether the shop accepts a documented alternative.

Because discovering a mysterious scratch when you're returning the bike is not how anybody wants to spend their afternoon.

#### DON'T BE THAT GUY // FILE 001

**“I DON'T NEED A HELMET. I'M ONLY GOING DOWN THE STREET.”**

Famous last words from somebody who apparently believes accidents require an appointment.

You can fall off a motorcycle fifty meters from your accommodation.

Distance doesn't cancel physics.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Wear a properly fitted, fastened helmet on every ride, even short ones.

#### DON'T BE THAT GUY // FILE 002

**“I'VE BEEN RIDING SCOOTERS FOR YEARS.”**

Excellent. We respect experience.

Now respect the fact that you're riding somewhere unfamiliar.

Your experience is an advantage, not diplomatic immunity from bad road conditions.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Ride defensively, adjust your speed to conditions, and never assume another driver has seen you.

#### DON'T BE THAT GUY // FILE 003

**“THE GUY AT THE RENTAL SHOP SAID I DON'T NEED A LICENSE.”**

That's wonderful.

Unfortunately, the rental shop doesn't write Thailand's traffic laws or your insurance policy.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Verify your legal riding eligibility, license category, permit requirements, and insurance before renting.

#### DON'T BE THAT GUY // FILE 004

**“IT'S JUST A LITTLE RAIN.”**

Sir, this is a tropical island.

That little rain can become a downpour before you've finished explaining why you're not worried.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Avoid riding in severe weather. Reduce speed when conditions deteriorate, and stop somewhere safe when necessary.

#### THE FINAL WORD

We're not here to lecture you or tell you how to spend your holiday.

You're an adult. You make your own decisions.

But we are going to give you the information you need to make those decisions intelligently.

Wear your helmet. Know the law. Get properly insured. Respect the roads. And don't let somebody else's questionable decisions become your personal standard.

You came to Lamai to train, explore, enjoy Thailand, and make memories.

**Not to become somebody's cautionary tale at the front desk.**

Enjoy the ride. Just make sure you get back in one piece.

### FIELD REPORT 004-02 // TAXIS & SONGTHAEWS — AGREE ON THE PRICE
**CAMP ADVICE**

Transportation around Samui doesn't always work the way it does back home.

Don't assume a street taxi will use a meter.

Confirm the price before the journey begins.

Songthaews, the shared pickup trucks used for local transport, can be a useful option. Confirm the route and fare before boarding, especially for private trips or late-night journeys.

Ride-hailing apps may also help where service is available.

**[NEW DRAFT] FIELD NOTE:** If you haven't agreed on a price, don't be surprised when you and the driver have two very different ideas about what the journey costs.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Confirm route, fare and payment method before traveling. No unverified fixed prices.

### FIELD REPORT 004-03 // OCEAN, FLAGS & MARINE LIFE
**[NEW DRAFT] · SAFETY NOTICE · CLINICAL REVIEW REQUIRED**

Samui's beaches are beautiful. The ocean is not a swimming pool with a scenic background.

Conditions can change, and a beach that looks inviting from the sand may have dangerous currents or marine hazards.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Follow warning flags and local beach guidance. Do not enter rough water or swim when warnings are in place. For suspected dangerous jellyfish stings or other serious marine injuries, seek emergency medical assistance immediately and follow locally approved, medically vetted first-aid guidance.

### FIELD REPORT 004-04 // SOI DOGS — GIVE THEM SPACE
**[NEW DRAFT] · SAFETY NOTICE**

You'll see dogs around neighborhoods and roads. Many are accustomed to people, but unfamiliar animals can behave unpredictably.

Don't approach, feed, corner, or provoke a dog you don't know.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Avoid contact with unfamiliar animals. If bitten or scratched, wash thoroughly with soap and running water and seek prompt medical assessment for rabies and infection risks.

### FIELD REPORT 004-05 // MOSQUITOES, DENGUE & SMALL CREATURES
**[NEW DRAFT] · SAFETY NOTICE**

Tropical islands have wildlife. Sometimes the wildlife has six legs and absolutely no respect for your personal space.

Mosquitoes deserve particular attention because dengue is a genuine concern. Some mosquitoes that spread dengue bite during the day.

You may also encounter centipedes, house geckos, and larger tokay geckos. Leave wildlife alone, shake out shoes stored outside, and avoid handling unfamiliar animals.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Use appropriate repellent, reduce exposed skin when practical, and seek medical advice for concerning bites, stings or illness.

### FIELD REPORT 004-06 // DRINKING WATER & ICE
**[NEW DRAFT] · SAFETY NOTICE**

Heat, training, and travel can leave you dehydrated faster than you expect.

Choose safe drinking water from reliable sources. Do not assume tap water is potable or that a particular ice shape guarantees safe handling.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Use sealed bottled water or another verified safe source, choose reputable food and drink vendors, and stay hydrated.

### FIELD REPORT 004-07 // RENTAL DEPOSITS & SMALL SCAMS
**[NEW DRAFT] · CAMP ADVICE**

A rental agreement is not the most exciting holiday reading. Neither is a surprise damage bill.

Take photographs before accepting equipment or a vehicle. Keep your receipts. Confirm deposits and return terms in writing.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Review terms before paying, document condition, and use traceable payments where practical.

### FIELD REPORT 004-08 // EMERGENCIES: READ THIS FIRST
**[NEW DRAFT] · SAFETY NOTICE · CONTACT VERIFICATION REQUIRED**

If somebody is seriously injured or in immediate danger, prioritize emergency assistance over reading a long field report.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Contact the appropriate local emergency service, give your location clearly, and follow professional instructions. Camp staff may assist when available but are not a substitute for emergency responders.

Your trainers are not here to validate every habit you arrived with.

Listen. Ask questions. Try the correction. And remember that the person holding pads for you may have spent much of their life learning this art.

Congratulations, you won Tuesday afternoon sparring against somebody who has a day job and came here to learn.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Match agreed intensity, protect your partner and follow your trainer's instructions.

**[NEW DRAFT] · CAMP ADVICE**

Your first week training in Thailand might feel different from your first week training back home.

That's because heat and humidity are not impressed by your fitness tracker.

**THE ACTUAL RULE:** Build up gradually, hydrate, rest when needed and tell staff if you feel unwell. Seek urgent medical care for severe heat-illness symptoms.

### FIELD REPORT 006-04 // CLEAN GEAR, CLEAN HABITS
**[NEW DRAFT] · CAMP ADVICE**

Your gloves, wraps, shin guards, and training clothes spend a lot of time around sweat.

They deserve a little attention.

You came here to get better. That doesn't mean every session needs to become a personal documentary about suffering.
